# FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

## our Process

- ### What sources does the NZA draw from?






The information contained in the National Zoning Atlas draws entirely from zoning codes, which are (or should be!) publicly available. We also call and confirm treatments with local officials where needed, and we keep an internal log of these communications.



Note that zoning codes are not always readily available. Our team spends significant time tracking down updated and accurate copies of zoning texts and maps. Sometimes, despite our best efforts, we can’t obtain them. We note these situations on our public map.

- ### How much interaction do you have with local governments?






We contact local jurisdictions when we have trouble finding an updated and accurate copy of the zoning code, when we have a question about how a code should be interpreted, or when we know a locality is undergoing a zoning code update. We very much welcome input, corrections, and feedback from local planners and officials about our map! [Contact us here](https://www.zoningatlas.org/contact).

- ### How do you keep the NZA updated?






Zoning codes are always changing. In some cases, changes result from shifts in local priorities. In other cases, changes result from information provided by the National Zoning Atlas itself. We’re glad to have spurred on zoning reforms, but it does mean that our work is never done.



For years, we’ve conducted spot-checks and patched up errors using staff with spare time between projects. But that approach is neither thorough nor sustainable — especially since we’ve found that updating sometimes takes nearly as much staff time as build-out.



So, at the urging of and with the support of some of our largest donors, we set up [Land Use Labs](https://www.landuselabs.com/) to license our data in 2026. This ensures that we can conduct the systematic, professional reviews that make the National Zoning Atlas accurate and up to date into the future. And it provides a sustainable funding model that continues to deliver the [public map](https://www.zoningatlas.org/atlas) and [Zoning Snapshots](https://www.zoningatlas.org/snapshots) as we always have.

- ### Do you use artificial intelligence or machine learning?






The team building the NZA does not use AI to read and interpret zoning texts. Through work with the Urban Institute and researchers at Cornell Tech, we have concluded that AI models simply cannot (yet) handle the nuances of lengthy, complex zoning codes to the level of accuracy we require. **_Anyone who tells you otherwise doesn’t understand zoning._**



Our founder wrote an article, “ [Keeping AI in its Zone](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7392558),” summarizing our approach.


## GETTING INVOLVED

- ### Can I volunteer with the NZA?






Thanks for your interest! We welcome people with a legal or planning background—and law firms seeking pro bono opportunities—to help us (a) ascertain whether jurisdictions have zoning, and (b) collect zoning codes. (This task is not as easy as it sounds!) Because it takes time to train and onboard volunteers, we ask that you commit to at least 40 hours over a three-month period. If this describes you, please [contact us](https://www.zoningatlas.org/contact) with your availability and interest.



We do not accept volunteers to help with the textual or geospatial analysis of zoning codes. Nor do we onboard new external institutional contributors.

- ### Can I download NZA data?






Yes, you can. Visit [Land Use Labs](https://www.landuselabs.com/), our licensing arm, which provides the sustainable funding model that continues to deliver the [public map](https://www.zoningatlas.org/atlas) and [Zoning Snapshots](https://www.zoningatlas.org/snapshots) as we always have.

- ### How can I get my city or town up on the map?






[Contact us](https://www.zoningatlas.org/contact) to ask if we have plans to analyze your city or town.


## Who we are:

Land Use Atlas, Inc., is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization that aims to illuminate critical information about the land use regulations that shape our lives.

LUAI houses the National Zoning Atlas, which is digitizing, demystifying, and democratizing information about zoning conditions in more than 33,000 jurisdictions in the United States — empowering advocates, revealing insights, and driving research. LUAI also stewards the National Preservation Atlas, which is documenting, illuminating, and accelerating historic preservation activity across the country.
